# Answered

## q-slot-shape — what is a time slot? ✅

**Answer (project owner, 2026-08-19):** a fixed grid. An assignment is `(technician, date, slot)`
with slot in `{morning, afternoon, evening}`. A job occupies exactly one slot; spanning consecutive
slots was considered and not chosen.

**Consequence — this is the important part:** no-double-booking is now enforceable *by the database*
as a **unique partial index on active assignments**, so two dispatchers racing each other is
resolved by the second write failing on a duplicate key, which the service translates into 409. The
index must be partial — superseded assignments must not block a slot (see `q-reassignment`).

A plain unique index here would be a bug: it would make a technician's slot permanently unusable
after any reassignment.

Source: interview §8.1. Unblocked: `ent-assignment`, `con-no-double-booking`,
`proc-assign-technician`.

## q-reassignment — can a job change technician? ✅

**Answer:** yes, with history. `Job ↔ Assignment` is **1:N with exactly one active**. Reassigning
marks the previous assignment `superseded` and creates a new `active` one. Rationale given:
technicians call in sick, and the history answers "who was meant to do this".

Source: interview §8.2. Unblocked: `rel-job-assignment`, `ent-assignment`.

## q-completed-correction — what if a completed job was wrong? ✅

**Answer:** a **dispatcher only** may reopen a completed job back to `in_progress`, recording a
reason and an actor. A technician cannot. If the job was already `invoiced`, **its invoice is voided
first**. Cancelling a completed job remains forbidden — reopening is a distinct transition with a
distinct owner.

Source: interview §8.3. Unblocked: `proc-job-lifecycle`. Created: `q-void-invoice-rules`,
`q-reopen-reinvoice`.

# Open

## Blocking the schema

### q-slot-capacity — is one job per slot absolute?

Can a dispatcher explicitly override and put two jobs in a technician's slot, or is it hard-limited?

**Why it matters more than it looks:** an override path would mean the unique index **cannot** be
the enforcement mechanism, which reverses the `q-slot-shape` decision and puts the concurrency
problem back in application code. Cheap to answer now, expensive after the index ships.

Blocks: `con-no-double-booking`, `ent-assignment`

### q-customer-deletion — can a customer be deleted with open jobs?

And can a job exist without a customer?

**Why:** decides required-ness and the delete strategy — block, cascade, or soft-delete. The wrong
choice orphans jobs, and it is discovered months later.

Blocks: `ent-customer`, `rel-customer-jobs`

### q-technician-deactivation — how does a technician leave?

And what happens to their already-scheduled future assignments?

**Why:** `Technician.active` is `assumed` — nobody stated it. Hard deletion would orphan assignment
history, so deactivation is likely, but unassigning their future work is a real workflow nobody has
described. Interacts with `q-reassignment`: those assignments presumably become superseded.

Blocks: `ent-technician`

## Blocking stories and acceptance criteria

### q-void-invoice-rules — who may void an invoice, and can a paid one be voided?

Reopening an invoiced job voids its invoice (§8.3), but nobody has said who else may void, whether a
reason is required, or what happens when the invoice is already marked **paid**.

**Why:** voiding a paid invoice is a money-affecting action with no stated owner. Left unanswered,
it will be implemented as whatever was easiest.

Blocks: `ent-invoice`, `proc-invoice-generation`

### q-reopen-reinvoice — what happens after a reopened job is completed again?

Does it generate a new invoice automatically, and what links the new invoice to the voided one?

**Why:** invoice generation currently assumes a completed job has no prior invoice. Without an
answer, a re-completed job either silently skips invoicing or creates an unlinked duplicate — and
both failures are invisible until someone reconciles the books.

Blocks: `proc-invoice-generation`, `ent-invoice`

### q-completion-requirements — does completing a job need more than notes?

A photo, a customer signature, parts used, time on site?

**Why:** each is a required field and an acceptance criterion. Photos additionally mean file upload,
which brings its own OWASP surface — size limits, content-type validation, storage location, and
whether the file is publicly addressable.

Blocks: `ent-job`, `proc-job-lifecycle`

### q-invoice-pricing — where do line prices come from?

Typed by the dispatcher, taken from a rate card, or derived from time on site and parts used?

**Why:** every attribute of `InvoiceLine` is `implied`. A rate card would be an entity nobody has
mentioned; deriving from time on site makes this depend on `q-completion-requirements`.

Blocks: `ent-invoice-line`, `proc-invoice-generation`

### q-cancel-reason — who can cancel, and must they give a reason?

**Why:** interview §3.3 names an owner for every transition **except** cancellation, and
`Job.cancelledReason` is `assumed`. If a technician can cancel, that is a third authorization rule.
Note §8.3 settled who may *reopen* but said nothing about who may *cancel*.

Blocks: `ent-job`, `con-ordered-transitions`

## Blocking compliance

### q-pii-retention — retention and residency for personal data?

Customer addresses and technician scheduling data are both personal data.

**Why:** GDPR applicability is unresolved in `docs/delivery/PROFILE.md`. Retention has to be decided
in `/design-schema` — retrofitting deletion onto a live dataset costs far more than choosing a
period before the first record exists.

Blocks: `ent-customer`, `ent-technician`, `AuditLog.source`

### q-audit-source-trust — how many proxy hops sit in front of the API?

`AuditLog.source` and the credential rate limiter both key on `req.ip`
(`server/src/lib/requestSource.js`). `trust proxy` is not configured (`server/src/app.js`), so that
value is the TCP peer.

**Why:** `docs/delivery/PROFILE.md` records delivery mechanism, backend host and transport as
**unknown — ask**. Behind a TLS terminator every client collapses to the terminator's address: the
limiter's 10-attempt bucket becomes global, and every audit entry attributes every failure to one
value. The fix is a validated hop count in `src/config/env.js` and `app.set('trust proxy', …)`, but
the count is a deployment fact, not a default — and `trust proxy: true` is actively unsafe, because
`X-Forwarded-For` then becomes client-controlled and an attacker can both evade the limiter and
write chosen strings into an append-only column.

Blocks: `AuditLog.source` attribution, `BE-01-02 AC-3` being provable in production
