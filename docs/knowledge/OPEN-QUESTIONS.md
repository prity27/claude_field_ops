## Blocking the schema

These decide fields, indexes or cardinality. `/design-schema` cannot be trusted past them.

### q-slot-shape — what is a time slot?

A fixed grid (hourly, or morning/afternoon), or an arbitrary start and end datetime per job?

**Why it is expensive:** it decides how the no-double-booking rule can be enforced *at all*. A fixed
grid makes it a unique index on `(technician, slot)` — the database enforces it, and concurrent
dispatchers are handled for free. Arbitrary windows make it a range-overlap query, which **no unique
index can express**, so the atomicity has to come from somewhere else. That is not a schema detail;
it is the core invariant of the product.

Blocks: `ent-assignment`, `con-no-double-booking`, `proc-assign-technician`

### q-reassignment — can a dispatched or in-progress job change technician?

And if so, is the original assignment replaced or kept as history?

**Why:** decides whether Job↔Assignment is 1:1 or 1:N-with-one-active. Changes the schema, the
overlap query, and at least two stories. Currently modelled as 1:1 with `confidence: implied` —
i.e. nobody said it.

Blocks: `rel-job-assignment`, `ent-assignment`

### q-customer-deletion — can a customer be deleted with open jobs?

And can a job exist without a customer?

**Why:** decides required-ness and the delete strategy — block, cascade, or soft-delete. The wrong
choice orphans jobs, and it is discovered months later.

Blocks: `ent-customer`, `rel-customer-jobs`

### q-technician-deactivation — how does a technician leave?

And what happens to their future assignments?

**Why:** `Technician.active` is currently `assumed` — nobody stated it. Hard deletion would orphan
assignment history, so deactivation is likely, but the unassignment of already-scheduled future work
is a real workflow nobody has described.

Blocks: `ent-technician`

## Blocking stories and acceptance criteria

### q-completion-requirements — does completing a job need more than notes?

A photo, a customer signature, parts used, time on site?

**Why:** each is a required field and an acceptance criterion. Photos additionally mean file upload,
which brings its own OWASP surface — size limits, content-type validation, storage location, and
whether the file is publicly addressable.

Blocks: `ent-job`, `proc-job-lifecycle`

### q-completed-correction — what happens when a completed job was wrong?

The stated lifecycle says a completed job cannot be cancelled (interview §3.2), and defines no
correction path.

**Why:** this is the gap most likely to be discovered in production. Without an in-system remedy,
the first real mistake is fixed by someone editing the database by hand — which is unlogged,
unreviewed, and sets the precedent.

Blocks: `proc-job-lifecycle`, `ent-invoice`

### q-invoice-pricing — where do line prices come from?

Typed by the dispatcher, taken from a rate card, or derived from time on site and parts used?

**Why:** every attribute of `InvoiceLine` is currently `implied`. A rate card would be an entity
nobody has mentioned; deriving from time on site makes this depend on `q-completion-requirements`.

Blocks: `ent-invoice-line`, `proc-invoice-generation`

### q-cancel-reason — who can cancel, and must they give a reason?

**Why:** interview §3.3 names an owner for every transition **except** cancellation, and
`Job.cancelledReason` is `assumed`. If a technician can cancel, that is a third authorization rule.

Blocks: `ent-job`, `con-ordered-transitions`

## Blocking compliance

### q-pii-retention — retention and residency for personal data?

Customer addresses and technician scheduling data are both personal data.

**Why:** GDPR applicability is unresolved in `docs/delivery/PROFILE.md`. Retention has to be decided
in `/design-schema` — retrofitting deletion onto a live dataset is far more expensive than choosing
a period before the first record exists.

Blocks: `ent-customer`, `ent-technician`
